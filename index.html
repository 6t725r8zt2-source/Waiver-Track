How the Waiver Skill metric works

Attribution. Every completed waiver claim or free-agent add starts a "stint" for that player on that team, which ends when the team drops or trades him. Any week he's in that team's starting lineup during the stint, his points get credited to that pickup. If you trade for a guy someone else picked up, you get no waiver credit. The original team stops getting credit once he's gone.

PAR (Points Above Replacement). Raw pickup points reward managers whose drafts went badly and who had to start pickups. PAR compares each start to a replacement-level starter at that position, that week, across the whole league (default: the 20th-percentile starter score). A pickup that scored 14 when replacement-level was 8 earns +6. Starting a waiver guy who did worse than replacement counts against you. So PAR measures finding and trusting the right guys.

  • Going rate = total PAR from every paid claim in the league ÷ total FAAB dollars bid. It's what an average FAAB dollar bought this season (shown at the top).
  • Value = PAR − (going rate × FAAB spent). How much more, or less, a manager got than their spending should have bought. Spending big for nothing is heavily negative; free pickups that hit are pure profit.
  • Skill Score = PAR + Value. It rewards both finding production and getting it cheaply, with no caps.
  • Hit rate = share of pickups that produced at least one start above replacement.
  • $/PAR = FAAB dollars spent per point above replacement (paid claims only). Lower is better.
  • Lineup share = % of the team's total starter points that came from pickups.

Caveats. A pickup who boomed on your bench gets no credit (the "Bench pts" column shows what you left there). Transactions are matched to weeks using Sleeper's week ("leg") field. Weeks still in progress use live partial scores.
